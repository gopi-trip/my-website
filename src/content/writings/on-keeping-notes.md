For years my notes were a graveyard. Highlights from books I'd half-read,
links I meant to come back to, screenshots of whiteboards whose meaning
evaporated by Monday. I was collecting, not thinking.

What changed wasn't a tool. It was a rule: **a note isn't done until it says
something in my own words that I could be wrong about.** A highlight can't be
wrong. A claim can.

## Three habits that stuck

- **Write the question first.** "What is KL divergence?" is a worse note than
  "Why is KL divergence asymmetric, and when does that bite?"
- **Link before you file.** If a new note doesn't connect to anything, either
  I don't understand it yet, or I've found something genuinely new. Both are
  worth knowing.
- **Revisit on purpose.** The wiki marks each note as a seedling, budding or
  evergreen. Seedlings are a to-do list I'm not allowed to ignore.

> The point of a garden isn't the harvest. It's that you keep showing up.

I don't always keep these habits. But the notes I wrote this way are the ones
I still open.
